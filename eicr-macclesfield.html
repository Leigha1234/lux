Frequently Asked Questions

EICR frequently asked questions

What is an Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR)?

An Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR) is a detailed inspection and test of a property's fixed electrical installation. It assesses the condition of wiring, sockets, switches, consumer units, earthing and protective devices to identify damage, deterioration, defects and potential safety risks. Lux Electrical Engineering Ltd provides professional EICR services for domestic, commercial and industrial properties.

How often should an EICR be carried out?

The recommended frequency depends on the type of property, its use and the condition of the electrical installation. As a general guide, domestic owner-occupied properties are often inspected every 10 years, while rented homes are generally inspected at least every 5 years or sooner if specified in the previous report. Commercial and industrial properties may require more frequent inspections depending on their environment and usage.

How long does an EICR take to complete?

The time required depends on the size of the property, the number of circuits and the complexity of the electrical installation. A typical domestic inspection may take a few hours, while larger homes and commercial or industrial premises can take considerably longer. We can provide a more accurate estimate once we understand the property and its electrical installation.

What happens if my EICR is unsatisfactory?

An unsatisfactory EICR means that one or more issues have been identified that require attention. These may include potentially dangerous defects, inadequate earthing or bonding, or problems with protective devices. Your report will identify the relevant observations and their classifications, helping you understand what remedial work is required. Lux Electrical Engineering Ltd can discuss the findings with you and provide a quotation for any necessary remedial work.

Do landlords legally need an EICR for rental properties?

In England, landlords are generally required to have the electrical installations in their rented properties inspected and tested by a qualified person at least every five years, or sooner if the report specifies. Landlords must obtain a report, provide it to existing tenants within the required timeframe and supply it to new tenants before occupation. Where the report identifies further investigative or remedial work, the applicable legal requirements and deadlines must also be met.
